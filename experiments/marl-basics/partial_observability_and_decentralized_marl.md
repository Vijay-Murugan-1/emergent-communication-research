# Partial Observability and Decentralized Multi-Agent Learning

## Objective

These experiments introduce the foundational concepts behind:

* partial observability,
* decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning,
* information asymmetry,
* local observations,
* and coordination under uncertainty.

The purpose of these experiments is to understand how incomplete information naturally creates the need for:

* coordination,
* information sharing,
* and communication between intelligent agents.

These concepts form one of the most important foundations behind:

* emergent communication systems,
* sender-receiver architectures,
* and decentralized MARL research.

---

# Local Observation Simulation

## Core Idea

In decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning, agents often observe only a small portion of the full environment.

Each agent possesses:

* limited local information,
* incomplete environmental knowledge,
* and partial observations.

---

## Main Understanding

Agents do not always have access to:

* the complete global environment state.

Instead:

* different agents observe different local regions or features.

This creates:

* uncertainty,
* incomplete information,
* and coordination challenges.

---

## Important Observations

* Different agents possess different observations.
* No individual agent fully understands the complete environment.
* Partial observability increases coordination difficulty.

---

# Information Asymmetry and Communication

## Core Idea

Different agents may possess different information.

One agent may know:

* important environmental details,
  while another agent lacks that information.

Communication improves coordination and task success.

---

## Main Understanding

Communication becomes useful when:

* information is distributed unevenly,
* and coordination improves rewards.

This creates the foundational intuition behind:

* emergent communication systems.

---

## Important Observations

* Agent B initially lacks critical information.
* Communication improves coordination quality.
* Information sharing increases successful task completion probability.

---

# Centralized vs Decentralized Observation

## Core Idea

This experiment compares:

* centralized systems,
* decentralized systems.

---

## Centralized Systems

A centralized controller observes:

* the complete environment state.

Decision making becomes easier because:

* full information is available.

---

## Decentralized Systems

Each agent only observes:

* local information,
* partial environmental data.

This creates:

* uncertainty,
* incomplete knowledge,
* and coordination complexity.

---

## Important Observations

* Centralized systems possess complete information.
* Decentralized agents operate under limited observations.
* Decentralized coordination becomes significantly harder.

---

# Coordination Under Uncertainty

## Core Idea

Agents must coordinate despite:

* incomplete information,
* local observations,
* and uncertain agent behavior.

---

## Main Understanding

Lack of coordination may create:

* collisions,
* failed tasks,
* inefficient behavior,
* and reduced rewards.

Coordination becomes significantly more difficult under:

* decentralized learning,
* and partial observability.

---

## Important Observations

* Incomplete information increases uncertainty.
* Poor coordination may reduce overall performance.
* Multi-agent systems require adaptive coordination strategies.

# Difference from Single-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Single-Agent RL:

* one agent interacts with the environment,
* environment remains relatively stable.

Decentralized Multi-Agent RL:

* multiple agents interact simultaneously,
* agents possess incomplete information,
* environment dynamics become more complex.

This creates:

* non-stationary learning,
* coordination challenges,
* and communication necessity.
